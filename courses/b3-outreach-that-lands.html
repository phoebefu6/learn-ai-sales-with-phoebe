Part 1 · covers the 2025-2026 cold-email benchmark reports

The ladder: five levers, one email 6 min live

Every lever is a different kind of homework, and they are not worth the same. Trigger event more than doubles the rate on its own because it is the only one that proves a human looked. The rest compound on top of it. The last rung, No AI tells, is the only one where you add nothing at all - you delete, and the number goes up by five points.

LiveWhat each lever actually costs you4 min▶

Reps skip levers by price, not by value. Here is the honest cost of each one:

LeverWhat it costsWhere it comes from
Trigger event4-6 minutes per prospectTheir news page, LinkedIn posts, job reqs, earnings call, a lease or permit filing
Named painOnce per segment, not per prospectYour last 10 discovery calls. The buyer's words, not your category's
Proof pointOnce per segmentOne comparable customer, one number, one time period
Specific askSeconds, once you stop asking for timeA question they can answer in one line from a phone
No AI tellsSeconds - you are deletingThe checklist in Part 2. Under 80 words

Two of the five are per-segment work you do once and reuse for a quarter. Only the trigger is genuinely per-prospect, and it is the one worth the most. That ratio is the entire argument for AI in outreach: let it do the finding, keep the writing.

LiveWhat AI drafts, what you write3 min▶

The working split that survives contact with a real week:

  • AI finds and summarises - scan the account's last 90 days, surface candidate triggers with dates and links, tell you which of your segment pains plausibly applies.
  • You choose the trigger and write the first line. This is the sentence the entire email lives or dies on. It is 20 words. Write it yourself.
  • AI tightens - cut to under 80 words, strip jargon, flag tells, offer three subject lines.
  • You own the ask and the send. Nothing goes out unread. Ever.
Prompt that works - finding, not writing 
Here is a company: Northwind Logistics, 3PL, Singapore, ~900 staff.
Find every dated, public thing that happened to them in the last 90 days:
funding, leases, facility openings, exec hires, job postings, earnings
remarks, product launches, regulatory filings.

For each: the date, one sentence of what happened, the source link, and
which of these three pains it plausibly creates:
(1) decisions lost between meetings during a ramp
(2) new sites out of sync with HQ
(3) new managers with no shared operating record

Rank by how recent and how specific. Do not write an email.
Prompt that produces the 2.8% email 
Write a cold email to the VP of Operations at Northwind Logistics
selling our AI meeting intelligence platform. Make it professional
and persuasive, and personalise it.

The second prompt has no trigger to work with, so the model fills the gap with the only thing it has: your product. That is the whole reason generated outreach sounds like generated outreach.

Part 2 · what the buyer sees

The eight tells, and the two seconds you get 6 min live

Your buyer does not read the email. She scans the first line, the shape of the paragraphs, and the ask. Every tell below is something she has now seen a hundred times this month, and each one is a signal that no human spent any time on her. The right-hand column is the same email once the tells are gone - shorter, more specific, and noticeably less polite.

Self-studyWhy "personalisation at scale" turned into an insult3 min read▶

The benchmark data genuinely supports personalisation: deeper personalisation and tighter cohorts lift reply rates by multiples, and advanced-personalisation campaigns report roughly double the replies of generic ones. Teams read that, buy a tool with forty merge fields, and get worse results. Here is the gap.

  • Depth means a human read something. One specific, dated, checkable fact. It cannot be produced by a field lookup because the value is the reading, not the data.
  • Merge fields simulate depth. "{{first_name}}, as a fellow {{industry}} leader in {{city}}" contains four facts and zero attention, and buyers have been trained by volume to spot the pattern instantly.
  • Empty fields are catastrophic, not cosmetic. The failure is visible to the buyer and unambiguous: she is a row in a spreadsheet, and now she has proof.

The anti-lever in tonight's simulator is calibrated to behave the way this actually behaves: it lowers the rate at every rung, and on the one prospect who stated her pain publicly it produces the worst outcome on the board.

Self-studyBefore you send: the rails that decide whether you may4 min read▶

Copy quality is a performance question. Whether you are allowed to send is a legal one, and it is not optional. The three regimes most likely to touch a B2B list, in plain terms:

RegimeApplies toThe rule that bites
PDPA (Singapore)Personal data of individuals in Singapore, plus the Do Not Call registry for phone numbersConsent or a valid exception for the data; check DNC before calling or texting a Singapore number
CAN-SPAM (US)Commercial email to US recipientsAccurate headers, no deceptive subject lines, a physical postal address, and a working opt-out honoured promptly
CASL (Canada)Commercial electronic messages to Canadian recipientsConsent-first, with limited implied-consent windows. Penalties are high and the burden of proof sits with the sender
GDPR (EU/UK)Personal data of people in the EU or UKYou need a lawful basis. B2B legitimate interest is possible but must be documented, balanced, and easy to object to
  • The suppression list is a rail, not a preference. A prior opt-out in your CRM must score zero sends, forever. That is the eighth row in tonight's sequence and the reason it is capped at 0%.
  • AI raises the stakes. Volume that used to take a week now takes a minute, so a compliance mistake that used to touch 40 people now touches 4,000.
  • Get it checked. This card is orientation, not legal advice - your list, your jurisdictions, your counsel. Leader session a5 covers the org-level version.

Real world

The three capped rows are where most outbound budget actually goes. Sending to wrong seats, under-ICP accounts, and stale lists is invisible on a dashboard that counts sends, and it is the reason platform-average reply rates fell from about 5.1% to about 3.4% while every team reported "more activity". Your reply rate is a targeting metric wearing a copywriting costume.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 40 min total

  • Take the five accounts from b2. Spend six minutes each finding one dated, public trigger. Write the date and the source link next to each. If you cannot find one in six minutes, that is data about the account.
  • Write one email per account at 5/5 on the levers, each under 80 words. Use the finding prompt from Part 1 - and write the first line yourself, always.
  • Run all five through the eight-tell checklist. Count how many tells survived your first draft. Most people find three.
  • Check your list against the rails: any Canadian recipients, any prior opt-outs, any DNC-registered Singapore numbers. Fix before sending, not after.
  • Send them. Log what came back in your CRM with the trigger you used - b8 turns that into a pattern and b9 turns it into a forecast.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which lever is genuinely per-prospect work, and therefore the one reps skip most?

Pain and proof are per-segment work you do once and reuse for a quarter. The trigger is the one that costs six minutes per prospect - and it is also the heaviest lever on the board, which is why "let AI find, you write" is the whole operating rule of this session.

2 · Your sequence average is stuck at 10.1% even with all five levers on. What is the most likely cause?

Three of the eight rows are capped by targeting, not writing. Reply rate is a targeting metric wearing a copywriting costume - which is why b2 comes before b3 in this track.

3 · A prospect opted out of your emails eight months ago and is still in the CRM. What is the correct handling?

Opt-outs do not expire on their own, and routing around one through another domain is worse, not cleverer. This is the single row in the simulator where 0% is the professional outcome - and AI-era volume is exactly why the discipline matters more than it used to.
